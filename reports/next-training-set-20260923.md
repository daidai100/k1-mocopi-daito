## Decision after a matched 125-update test

**Do not extend either data-only arm.** The original 399-core allocation below gives 25% target exposure to clips selected mainly by geometric and reference-speed checks. A [training-only initializer replay of all 399 core originals](../artifacts/recommended-training-set-20260923/initializer-core-screen-summary.json) found **267/399 raw completions, 13 jointly clean clips, 132 falls, and 166 collision trials**. These are policy-specific diagnostics, not physics qualification.

I tested a narrower [focused pilot v2 manifest](../artifacts/recommended-training-set-20260923/focused-pilot-v2.json): 76 high-weight originals from 18 related take families, selected from 74 collision-free, speed/range-safe initializer replays plus two walks previously learned by the six-run campaign. It kept all **18,054 originals, zero mirrors**, with target mass of 10% on three learned anchors, 15% on 25 screened left clips, 15% on 48 screened right clips, 10% on remaining locomotion, and 50% on whole-body retention. The existing curriculum loader accepted the exact positive per-ID weights and source order. Both this arm and a fresh unchanged 50/50 full-corpus control passed separate 25-update GPU preflights using a [preprocessing-parity-rebound packed cache](../artifacts/recommended-training-set-20260923/focused-pilot-v2-cache.json) on local NVMe. Neither preflight weights nor optimizer state entered production.

Both production arms started from the same retained `guard_world` update-125 model and normalizers, with fresh optimizer and physics state, seed 42, the same RTX 5070 Ti/native MuJoCo backend, `world-body-v1` reward, preview, controller limits and 50/25/25 reset mix. Each completed **125 new updates / 8.192 million transitions / 8,000 Adam steps**, with finite updates and checkpoint reload error zero. The [recounted development replay and decision record](../artifacts/recommended-training-set-20260923/pilot-v2-assessment.json) used the same frozen source and corrected 63-original panel, without trial resets or execution errors:

| Policy | Raw / 63 | Historical clean | World + safety clean | Collisions | Falls | Jointly clean ordinary walks / 12 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Retained initializer | 30 | 18 | 13 | 14 | 33 | 0 |
| Focused v2, update 125 | 30 | 18 | 13 | **21** | 33 | 0 |
| Unchanged full-corpus control, update 125 | 30 | 19 | 13 | 18 | 33 | 0 |

The focused arm also regressed on its own 76 training diagnostics: **75 → 74 raw, 13 → 10 jointly clean, and 1 → 13 collision trials**. One left-labeled training clip became jointly clean, but five left training clips newly collided; this is neither held-out improvement nor a safety gain. Training-clip action blending with the initializer still produced 9–16 collision trials across the tested blends, so simple output averaging did not repair the regression. No focused checkpoint is promoted, no arm proceeds to update 250, and the 75-motion confirmation panel remains untouched.

## What must change for the next run

This is the earlier mechanism proposal, **deferred for the authorized five-run launch above** because that launch limits treatment changes to reward weights or data scheduling. None of the contact, reset, controller or reference contracts below is changed in those five runs.

The [new-collision contact census](../artifacts/recommended-training-set-20260923/collision-census.json) found ankle-against-ankle contact in **14 of 21** newly colliding training/development clips; the others include ankle/knee and arm/leg contacts. These are measured 500 Hz contact pairs. Current PPO's collision signal penalizes contact after it occurs. A versioned, bounded **precontact foot-clearance intervention** is the first safety hypothesis to test, with arm/leg pairs separately audited. Tune it only on training clips, replay the retained initializer and focused checkpoint under the identical intervention, and reject it if collisions disappear by worsening full-clip tracking, falls, joint limits, or speed. Do not relax the zero-collision evaluation gate or change the original references.

The training task also ends an episode on coarse tracking error and then resets physics to a reference state. The focused arm's last ten updates averaged **3.31 seconds per ended episode**, while its 76 selected clips have a **5.26-second median** duration. This is a plausible cause of the gap between short training episodes and uninterrupted benchmark replay, not a demonstrated sole cause. Implement a separately versioned recovery treatment that records the tracking failure, continues for a bounded recovery window from the **actual simulated state**, and terminates immediately for falls or unsafe state. Keep the full failed tail in reward and metrics; test that reset, reward, checkpoint and causal observation semantics remain valid. Log true start/full-clip exposure and per-stratum/take exposure, which the current native-family telemetry does not provide.

Only after both mechanisms pass train-only canaries should the next **paired 125-update experiment** begin. Use the unchanged 18,054-original 50/50 full-corpus weights, the same corrected references, initializer, reward, actuator limits, seed and GPU for both arms. Give both arms the verified contact intervention; compare existing phase-reset training against the actual-state recovery treatment. Preflight each for 25 finite updates/reload, then replay the same no-reset development panel and training diagnostics at matched transitions and actual Adam steps. Continue to 250/500 only if ordinary-walk full-duration fidelity improves **and** raw completion, protected clean pass identities, collisions and falls do not regress against the matched control and retained initializer. Require two new jointly clean ordinary walks before checkpoint qualification, then repeat a promising treatment at a second seed and use the untouched 75-motion panel once. The controller, reference and safety contracts must be versioned together; no new learner under this treatment has been launched.

## Earlier broad-core draft

The earlier draft starts from the [corrected, source-order-preserving library](../artifacts/next-policy-plan-20260922/clock-repaired-library-source-order-v2/index.jsonl) of **18,054 train originals, zero mirrors** (18,003 BONES-SEED and 51 KIT). It gives every original positive target weight. The previous eight-clip run learned its three selected walks but lost held-out locomotion and broad-motion behavior; another narrow walking-only continuation is not an acceptable production set. This draft would change sampling weights and group assignments, while preserving references, admission decisions, actuator limits, controller architecture, and evaluation thresholds. Its exact IDs and weights are in [the original selection ledger](../artifacts/recommended-training-set-20260923/proposal.json); measured walk features are in [the feature ledger](../artifacts/recommended-training-set-20260923/strict-walk-features.jsonl). Those weights remain a design artifact, not an executable curriculum manifest.

The core's strict 398 clips span **74 capture groups and 89 actors**. Their motion names begin `walk_ff_`, `walk_arc_`, `turn_walk_`, or `walk_180_`, avoiding obstacle, prop, dog-walking, and conversation motions in the high-weight tier. Each has 2–15 seconds of original 50 Hz playback, at least 0.5 m of root path, mean horizontal root speed 0.15–0.8 m/s, p95 horizontal speed at most 1.4 m/s, p95 absolute joint speed at most 4 rad/s, zero rejected ticks, and a passed independent 500 Hz geometric audit. The extra bounded-ground clip is the previously admitted `walk_ff_loop_180_R_slow` original; it passed full training-clip replay under several learned actors. That replay is policy-specific evidence, not physics qualification. Its separate status is explicit in the selection ledger.

The core contains loops, starts, stops, arcs, and turns. The three walks used in the eight-clip campaign are included, but now share weight with 396 additional originals. Within the core's 25% target mass, give each of its 37 related take families equal mass, then divide within each family by clip. The same core had **5.17%** nominal target mass in the previous full-corpus curriculum. Within the remaining 25% locomotion mass, assign 10% to the other 1,602 native walks, 5% to runs, 5% to turns, and 5% to the remaining walk-named or semantically locomotor clips. Preserve the previous curriculum's relative weights inside those subgroups and the 50% whole-body stratum. The draft's nominal native-walk mass is **14.73%**, versus **14.82%** previously; run and turn mass rise to 5% each. These are target transition shares, not measured exposure in a learner.

The take-family counts in the table are **per stratum**, not additive: there are 1,630 unique families overall, and 29 span strata. In particular, 26 core families also have clips in other locomotion, so balancing within the core does not equalize their *total* mass across the set. In the draft weights, those 37 core families receive 26.15% combined mass after their other-locomotion clips are included, compared with 25% from the core alone. Before freezing a curriculum, audit total per-family and left/right exposure across all strata and decide whether to cap cross-stratum duplication. Keep mirrors out of independent family counts.

The available core is directionally uneven: **32 right-labeled versus five left-labeled take families**. This reflects the corrected source pool, not a claim of balanced directional coverage. The other-locomotion stratum retains the remaining original walks, including left turns; report left/right outcomes separately. If the dynamic gate rejects most of the five left families, add or repair left-walk references before training rather than letting right variants stand in for them. Mirrors, if later validated as augmentation, must not be counted as new independent takes.

This is a **candidate set, not a cleared training launch**. All 18,054 rows are RL reference-admitted and none is marked physics-qualified. Geometric validity and moderate reference speeds do not establish contact or actuator feasibility. Before a long run, audit all 399 core clips at original timing against independent contact, torque-speed, stance-slip, and full-clip continuity checks. Risk-screen the other 17,655 sampled clips by stratum as well; passing the core audit would not qualify the rest. Keep a versioned reject ledger and preserve the existing safety and admission limits. A proposed breadth gate is at least **25 of the 37 distinct core take families**, including **at least four of the five left-labeled families**, covering loops, starts, stops, and arcs/turns. This threshold is a predeclared coverage requirement, not a measured feasibility result. If it fails, repair or replace the reference/authority problem before spending training compute; do not quietly shrink back to a handful of walks. Any rejected clip requires a new versioned ID/weight ledger and recalculated exposure; the current draft cannot be used unchanged after rejection.

The corrected 63-motion development panel and reserved 75-motion confirmation panel have zero normalized take-family overlap with this earlier draft. Its 399-core weights were never implemented or launched; the matched focused-v2 result above supersedes it as the first data-only test. Neither its nominal 25% core target nor a successful training-clip replay establishes generalization or dynamic qualification.
